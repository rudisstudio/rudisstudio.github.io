Let's Smash privacy policy

Effective date: 12 November 2026

Let's Smash ("the game") is made by Rudis Studio ("we"). This policy explains what happens to your information when you play Let's Smash: in the Android app, and in a web browser on CrazyGames ("the web version"). Where a section applies to only one of them, it says so.

Your use of the game is also covered by the Rudis Studio terms of use.

In short

We do not run any servers, the game needs no account, and we do not sell any personal information.

In the Android app, the game shows rewarded ads from Google AdMob only when you choose to watch one. It uses Google Firebase to send us gameplay statistics and crash reports, which are not linked to your name, email address or advertising ID. You can buy coins and bundles with real money; Google Play handles the payment, and the in-app purchase software from Unity that is built into the game sends Unity a record of each purchase, as described below. Apart from purchases and ads, the Android app plays fully offline.

In the web version, there are no ads, no purchases, and no gameplay statistics or crash reports. Your progress is saved through CrazyGames, as described under "Playing on the web (CrazyGames)".

Information we collect

The game does not send us your name, email address, precise location or contacts. We receive information only in these cases:

  • When you buy something in the Android app: Google Play gives us the order records we need for refunds and our accounts, such as the order number, the item, the price and the country of purchase. We do not receive your payment card details.
  • When you play the Android app: gameplay statistics and crash reports, through Google Firebase, as described under "Gameplay statistics and crash reports".
  • When you email us: see "Contacting support" below.

The web version sends us nothing. What Unity receives when you buy something in the Android app is described under "In-app purchases".

Information kept on your device (Android app)

To let you pick up where you left off, the Android app saves some data in the game's private storage on your phone:

  • your progress: the level you are on, your coins, your lives with the time your next life is due, your rocket uses, and any unlimited-lives time you have left;
  • a record of the purchases the game has already given you (the purchase's transaction number and how many times each item was bought), so that each purchase is given to you exactly once;
  • your settings: music, sound and vibration on or off;
  • whether you last chose the rocket booster before a level, and which new pieces and tips the game has already shown you.

The saved progress carries a signature so the game can tell if it was changed outside the game. In the Android app, this data is not sent to us or anyone else. Uninstalling the game, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, deletes it. Your phone's own system backup may include this data if you have Android backup turned on; that backup is handled by Google under your Google account's settings, not by us.

How the web version keeps your progress is described in the next section.

Playing on the web (CrazyGames)

You can also play Let's Smash in a web browser on CrazyGames, a games website run by CrazyGames. The web version is the same game without the shop: it has no in-app purchases and shows no ads, and it does not include Google Firebase, Google's ad software or Unity's in-app purchase software, so the information described under "In-app purchases", "Gameplay statistics and crash reports" and "Ads" is not collected there. The web version includes the CrazyGames SDK, software from CrazyGames, and uses it for these things:

  • Your progress: the same progress as in the Android app (the level you are on, your coins, your lives with the time your next life is due, your rocket uses, any unlimited-lives time you have left, and a few records the game keeps for itself, such as whether you have already received your free rocket uses), with the same signature, is saved through the CrazyGames SDK. If you play as a guest, CrazyGames keeps it in your browser's storage. If you are signed in to a CrazyGames account, CrazyGames keeps it with your account, so you can continue on other devices where you sign in; if you sign in after playing as a guest, CrazyGames moves your guest progress to your account.
  • If the CrazyGames SDK has not answered within 8 seconds when the game starts, the game saves that session's progress only in your browser's own storage, and does not join it with the progress saved through CrazyGames later.
  • Your settings and tips: music and sound on or off, whether you last chose the rocket booster, and which new pieces and tips the game has already shown you are kept in your browser's own storage, not through the SDK.
  • When you are playing: the game tells the SDK when play starts and when it stops, for example when a level begins or ends, or when you open the menu.
  • Your language: the game reads your language setting from the SDK, to show the game in your language.

None of this is sent to us. CrazyGames runs the website and the SDK, and handles the information they collect and store, including your saved progress, under the CrazyGames privacy policy.

In-app purchases (Android app)

You can play the whole game without paying. In the Android app, if you choose to, you can buy coin packs and bundles inside the game. Purchases are optional and need an internet connection. The web version has no purchases.

Google Play handles every purchase: you pay through your Google account, with the payment method you have set up in Google Play. The game and we never see your card or bank details. Google handles this information under Google's privacy policy, and your purchases appear in your Google Play order history.

Unity: the Android app uses Unity In-App Purchasing, software from Unity Technologies, to talk to Google Play. When you make a purchase, this software sends Unity a record of it, encrypted in transit:

  • the item bought and the purchase's transaction number;
  • Google Play's receipt for the purchase;
  • an installation identifier that Unity creates for the game on your phone, and a session identifier;
  • technical details of your device and its operating system, the game's version and when it was installed;
  • your consent settings (the game does not grant Unity advertising or analytics consent, so these are not granted).

Unity's servers may also estimate your approximate location (your country) from your IP address. Unity uses this information to run its in-app purchase service and for analytics about it. Unity sends these records whenever a purchase is made; there is no setting in the game to turn them off, so if you do not want them sent, please do not make purchases. They do not include your name, your email address, your payment details or your advertising ID. We do not receive this information from Unity. Unity handles it under the Unity privacy policy.

Gameplay statistics and crash reports (Android app)

The Android app uses two Google services, Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics, to tell us how the levels play and when the game crashes, so that we can fix problems and balance the levels. Google runs them for us. Both need an internet connection and send their data encrypted in transit. The web version does not include them.

Firebase Analytics sends:

  • what happens in the game: when you start, win or lose a level, with the level number and its difficulty, whether you used the rocket booster, the coins a win paid, whether you took a continue offer, and when you start, finish or cancel a purchase, with the item and where in the game you bought it;
  • events Firebase records on its own, such as the first time the game is opened, when a play session starts, how long the game is in use, and which screen is showing;
  • an app instance identifier that Firebase creates for the game on your phone;
  • technical details: the device model, the operating system version, the game's version, the language and your approximate location (country or region) estimated from your IP address.

Firebase Crashlytics sends a report when the game crashes or hits an error: what the game was doing when it failed (a technical trace of the code), the device model, the operating system and game versions, the free memory and storage, the time of the crash and an installation identifier that Crashlytics creates for the game on your phone.

The game does not give Firebase your name, email address, progress save or advertising ID: it turns off Firebase's collection of the advertising ID and does not allow this information to be used for ads. We do not use it to identify you, and we do not share it with advertisers. Google handles it as our service provider, under the Firebase privacy and security information and Google's privacy policy.

These statistics and crash reports are sent whatever you choose in the ad consent form; there is no setting in the game to turn them off. Uninstalling the game, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, stops them and replaces the identifiers with new ones.

Ads (Android app)

The Android app shows only rewarded ads, and only when you ask for one: to get an extra life when you have run out, or an extra use of the rocket booster. There are no banner ads and no ads that interrupt play. You can play the whole game without watching any ads, and players who have bought anything in the game are not offered ads. The web version shows no ads.

The ads are served by Google AdMob, through the Google Mobile Ads SDK built into the app. When the app starts and has an internet connection, this SDK connects to Google and prepares an ad, even if you never watch one. To do this, Google collects and shares the following information automatically, encrypted in transit:

  • IP address, which may be used to estimate your device's general location (not your precise location);
  • app interactions, such as app launches, taps and ad views;
  • diagnostic information, such as app launch time, hang rate and energy use;
  • device identifiers, such as the Android advertising ID and the app set ID.

Google uses this information to show and measure ads, for analytics about ad delivery, and to prevent fraud and abuse. We do not receive this information in a form that identifies you. Google handles it under Google's privacy policy; see also How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Your choices about ads (Android app)

In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland: before any ad is loaded, Google's consent form asks whether Google may use your information for personalised ads. You can choose not to consent. Google may then still show non-personalised ads, which use less information (for example your general location from the IP address), and it still uses some information to measure ads and prevent fraud. You can change your choice at any time in the game under Settings > Privacy options.

Everywhere: you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your phone's settings (depending on the phone, under Settings > Privacy > Ads or Settings > Google > Ads).

Contacting support

The Support button in the game's Settings opens your own email app with a new message to us. Before you send it, the message already contains the model of your device, its operating system (in the web version, as your browser reports them), the game's version and your device's language, so we can look into problems; you can read or remove these lines before sending. Nothing is sent unless you send the email yourself. We use what you send only to answer you, and you can ask us to delete your messages at any time.

The Terms and Privacy buttons open our terms of use and this policy in your web browser. The Privacy options button, shown only in the Android app where the consent form applies, opens Google's consent form again.

Permissions

The Android app asks Android for these permissions:

  • Vibration: gives short buzzes when the cannon fires, when a rocket or TNT crate goes off, and when you win. The vibration effects run on your phone and send nothing. You can turn vibration off in the game's Settings.
  • Internet and network state: used for in-app purchases, ads, gameplay statistics and crash reports, to reach Google Play, Google's ad servers and Firebase, and to check whether the phone is online.
  • Google Play billing: lets the game offer purchases through Google Play.
  • Advertising ID: lets Google's ad software read the Android advertising ID, as described under Ads.
  • A few system permissions added by Google's ad and Firebase software for its own work, such as Android's ad services (Privacy Sandbox) and keeping short background tasks running.

The game does not use your precise location, your camera, your microphone, your contacts or your files. The web version asks your browser for no permissions and does not vibrate.

Third parties

Besides the purchase software, Google's ad software and Firebase in the Android app, and the CrazyGames SDK in the web version, all described above, Let's Smash contains no third-party analytics or tracking code. Both versions are built with the Unity game engine, made by Unity Technologies. When the game starts with an internet connection, the engine may send Unity a small set of technical information, such as the device model, operating system, screen and graphics hardware, and the engine version. This does not include your name or your progress in the game, and we do not receive it. Unity handles it under the Unity privacy policy.

When you download the Android app from Google Play, Google may collect information as part of providing the store, under Google's privacy policy. When you play the web version, the CrazyGames website may collect information as part of providing its service, under the CrazyGames privacy policy.

Children

Let's Smash is meant for players aged 13 and over. It is not directed at children under 13 and is not listed for children on Google Play. If you believe a child under 13 is using the game, please contact us.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the Android app deletes everything it saved on your phone. We delete your support emails on request. We keep Google Play's order records only as long as we need them for refunds and our accounts, or as the law requires.

In the web version, if you play as a guest, clearing your browser's stored data (cookies and site data) for the CrazyGames website deletes your progress, settings and the record of tips shown. If you are signed in to a CrazyGames account, your progress is kept by CrazyGames with your account; the game cannot delete it, and you can ask CrazyGames about it as described in the CrazyGames privacy policy.

The purchase records described under "In-app purchases" are held by Unity, not by us; the game cannot delete them. To ask Unity about them, or to ask for their deletion, follow the instructions in the Unity privacy policy. If you need help, email us and we will help you make the request. Your Google Play purchase history is managed in your Google account.

Gameplay statistics and crash reports are kept in Firebase only as long as we need them to improve the game, and Google deletes them after the retention period we set. They are linked only to the identifiers described above, not to your name or email address, so we cannot find the data of one player; uninstalling the game or clearing its storage gives it new identifiers, so new data is no longer linked to the old.

Information Google collects for ads is held by Google; you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings, and manage it under Google's privacy policy.

Changes to this policy

If a future version changes how information is handled, for example by adding another ad partner or analytics service, or ads in the web version, we will update this policy and its effective date before that version is released.

The latest change added the web version on CrazyGames.
